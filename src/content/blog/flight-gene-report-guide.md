A flight-gene report arrives with genotype letters next to gene names — but what should you actually do with it? The honest answer: treat each line as a breeding reference with a known evidence limit, not as a race prediction. This guide walks through the panel and how to weigh each result.

## The 8 loci and what published research suggests

| Gene | Biological role | What research suggests | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| LDHA | Energy metabolism | Association with racing survival | B |
| DRD4 | Dopamine signalling | Association with racing performance | B |
| CRY1 | Circadian rhythm | Association with racing performance | B |
| LRP8 | Lipid transport | Association with racing performance | B |
| GSR | Antioxidant defence | Association with racing performance | B |
| CASK | Synaptic function | Polymorphism identification | C |
| MSTN | Muscle development regulation | Limited pigeon-specific evidence | D |
| F-KER | Feather keratin | Polymorphism identification | D |

Evidence grades follow the knowledge-base standard: **A** = multiple independent studies agree; **B** = pigeon-specific, peer-reviewed, statistically significant; **C** = single or preliminary pigeon study; **D** = biologically plausible with limited pigeon evidence. Each gene page links to the verifiable sources (PMID/DOI).

## What a genotype line means — and does not mean

A genotype such as "LDHA: GG" records which variants a bird carries at one locus. Research then describes how different genotypes distribute across performance groups in published samples. That is a **statistical association**, not a guarantee:

- Genotypes reflect genetic tendency only
- Race results are the product of many genes plus training, environment, nutrition and health
- One favourable marker never outweighs poor conditioning or loft management

This is why reports state that results support breeding reference, not result prediction.

## Three ways to use the report well

1. **Pairing decisions** — combine genotypes across loci when choosing mates, alongside pedigree and race records.
2. **Benchmarking** — keep genotype data in the bird's file and compare against actual performance over seasons.
3. **Early screening** — record squab genotypes shortly after hatching; DNA sequence is stable for life.

Feathers with intact calamus (3–5) or blood cards are both accepted sample types.

## The boundaries, in plain terms

- **Association is not causation.** A statistical link does not mean the gene "causes" better racing.
- **Evidence grades matter.** Treat D-grade markers (MSTN, F-KER) as identification tools, not performance signals.
- **Population context matters.** Published findings come from specific populations; effects may differ across strains and conditions.

## Bottom line

A flight-gene panel is best treated as structured breeding information: verified associations, graded evidence, and clear limits. Use it to inform pairings and record-keeping — then let the race basket remain the final judge.
