Sample collection is the "first step" of DNA testing, and sample quality directly determines the accuracy of the result. Racing pigeon DNA testing uses three common sample types — feathers, blood cards and oral swabs — each with its own use cases and precautions.

## Comparing the Three Sample Types

### Feather Samples

- **Suitable for**: adult and young pigeons, the most commonly used non-invasive sampling method;
- **Requirement**: must include a **complete feather root (follicle)**; the shaft alone contains no DNA;
- **Advantages**: non-invasive, easy to collect, can be stored at room temperature;
- **Disadvantages**: a detached or contaminated feather root will affect DNA extraction.

### Blood Card Samples

- **Suitable for**: situations requiring a larger amount of DNA or higher-precision testing;
- **Requirement**: blood must soak evenly through the blood card, then air-dry naturally before sealing;
- **Advantages**: high DNA content, good stability;
- **Disadvantages**: an invasive sampling method that requires careful technique.

### Oral Swab Samples

- **Suitable for**: parentage testing, DNA ID cards and similar applications;
- **Requirement**: use a sterile swab to scrape oral mucosal cells thoroughly, avoiding contamination;
- **Advantages**: non-invasive, simple to perform;
- **Disadvantages**: relatively few cells, so thorough scraping is required.

## Key Sampling Rules

1. **One person, one pigeon, one tube**: avoid cross-contamination between samples; number and seal each pigeon's sample separately;
2. **Avoid direct contact**: the sampler's hands must not touch the swab tip or feather root, to prevent contamination with human DNA;
3. **Label clearly**: mark the sample tube with the pigeon's number, ring number and sampling date;
4. **Ship promptly**: send the sample to the laboratory as soon as possible after collection; avoid prolonged storage at high temperatures;
5. **Store dry**: wet samples easily grow bacteria and degrade DNA, so make sure samples are dry before sealing.

## Common Sampling Mistakes

- **Pulling only the feather shaft without the root**: the shaft contains no DNA, so extraction will fail;
- **Mixing samples together**: samples from several pigeons placed in one tube cannot be traced to an individual;
- **Reusing a swab**: sampling several pigeons with one swab causes cross-contamination;
- **Storing samples damp or mouldy**: this harms the quality of DNA extraction.

## Why Correct Sampling Matters So Much

DNA testing is precision work where a tiny difference makes a huge difference. A contaminated, degraded or mixed-up sample can at best make a result unreadable, and at worst lead to an incorrect parentage determination or identity check.

Correct sampling plus rigorous laboratory quality control are the two gates that keep a result trustworthy.

## A Real Case: Two Sampling Results from the Same Pigeon

A fancier once sent "down feathers pulled off casually" in for parentage testing; the laboratory reported that the sample had no feather root and DNA extraction failed, so it had to be resubmitted. The same pigeon passed first time when a wing feather with a complete root was used instead. The case shows that **sample quality directly determines whether a test can return a result at all**.

Common failure scenarios and what to do instead:

| Scenario | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Sending down or fluffy feathers | No feather root, insufficient DNA | Use a wing or breast feather with a complete root |
| Blood card exposed to moisture | Microbial contamination, DNA degradation | Air-dry before bagging, add a desiccant |
| Swab wrapped in cling film | No ventilation, prone to mould | Use a breathable paper bag or a dedicated sample tube |
| Several pigeons packed together | Cross-contamination | One pigeon per bag, numbered on the outside |
