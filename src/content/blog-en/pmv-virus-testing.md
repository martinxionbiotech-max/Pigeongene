Paramyxovirus (PMV-1) is one of the most serious viral diseases affecting racing pigeons, commonly known as pigeon Newcastle disease. It has a high mortality rate and spreads quickly, and an outbreak can deal a devastating blow to a loft. **Early detection and strict control** are the key to protecting a racing pigeon colony.

## How PMV Spreads

PMV virus spreads mainly through the following routes:

- **Direct contact**: an infected pigeon contacts a healthy one, transmitting the virus through secretions and droppings;
- **Airborne spread**: the virus can travel short distances in droplets;
- **Fomite spread**: drinkers, feed troughs, cages and transport crates become transmission vehicles once contaminated;
- **Wild bird spread**: wild birds can act as carriers of the virus.

## Typical Clinical Signs

Racing pigeons infected with PMV usually show the following signs:

- **Neurological signs**: head twisting, circling, drooping wings, unsteady gait;
- **Digestive system**: green watery diarrhoea, reduced appetite;
- **Respiratory system**: difficulty breathing, sneezing, weeping eyes;
- **Sudden death**: some cases die rapidly without obvious warning signs.

Note that these signs are not specific to PMV, so a confirmed diagnosis must rely on laboratory testing.

## Testing Methods

Laboratory testing is the accepted reference method for confirming PMV:

- **qPCR nucleic acid testing**: high sensitivity and high specificity, able to detect viral nucleic acid in the early stage of infection (before symptoms appear);
- **Serological testing**: measures antibody levels, used to assess immune status and infection history;
- **Virus isolation**: used as a reference method for confirmation.

qPCR testing is recommended before races, when introducing new pigeons, and whenever suspicious signs appear, so that cases are found, isolated and handled early.

## Integrated Prevention and Control

Effective control requires several measures working together:

1. **Vaccination**: vaccinate against Newcastle disease on schedule to build flock immunity;
2. **Regular testing**: screen for the virus before races and before introducing new birds;
3. **Strict isolation**: newly introduced racing pigeons must be kept separate, observed and cleared by testing before joining the flock;
4. **Hygiene and disinfection**: disinfect the loft and equipment regularly;
5. **Biosecurity**: control the movement of people and vehicles, and prevent contact with wild birds.

## Conclusion

The core of PMV paramyxovirus control is prevention rather than reaction. Regular qPCR screening together with sound biosecurity management can markedly reduce the risk of an outbreak and protect both the health of your racing pigeons and their race results.
