The authenticity of a valuable racing pigeon's bloodline is a central concern in both trade and breeding. Traditional ring-number registration carries the risk of forgery or mix-ups, whereas a **DNA ID**, based on each pigeon's unique genetic fingerprint, provides lifelong, forgery-proof identification.

## What a DNA ID Is

A DNA ID tests multiple STR (short tandem repeat) microsatellite loci in the pigeon genome to build a DNA fingerprint unique to each bird. The number of repeats at STR loci varies widely between individuals, and in combination they are enough to tell birds apart.

## Core Applications

- **Identity verification**: confirm that "this bird" is the one in the record, preventing swaps;
- **Parentage testing**: confirm parent-offspring relationships and verify the accuracy of a pedigree;
- **Trade assurance**: provide genetic-level credentials for transactions involving valuable birds;
- **Breeding management**: build complete genetic records for breeding stock and support pairing decisions.

## How the Test Works

STR microsatellites are short sequences in the genome that repeat 2-6 bases. Multiple STR loci are amplified by PCR and the repeat count at each locus is measured, producing the individual's genotype combination. Parentage testing follows Mendelian inheritance: every STR locus in the offspring must be inherited from the parents, one copy from each, and comparing the profiles allows the parentage probability to be calculated.

## Test Workflow

1. **Sampling**: collect feathers with the root intact, a blood card, or an oral swab;
2. **DNA extraction**: obtain high-quality genomic DNA;
3. **STR typing**: genotype the standard set of STR loci;
4. **Comparison and analysis**: build the fingerprint profile, or run a parentage comparison;
5. **Report**: issue a formal report with the fingerprint profile attached.

## DNA ID vs Other Tests

| Test type | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| DNA ID | Which bird is this? |
| Parentage testing | Are they parent and offspring? |
| Flight ability gene testing | What is its genetic potential? |
| Pathogen testing | Is it healthy? |

## Case in Practice: Verifying Identity After an Auction Purchase

**Scenario**: a fancier paid a high price at auction for a "famous bloodline" breeder and wanted to confirm that the pedigree the seller provided actually matched the bird; he also planned to pair it with his best hen.

**How it was used**:

1. **Create a DNA ID**: a baseline record (STR markers) was created for the new bird, so its identity can be traced through molting, injury or a change of owner;
2. **Verify bloodline consistency**: if the seller can provide DNA records for the parents or related birds, a parentage comparison can check the pedigree records;
3. **Check relatedness before pairing**: an inbreeding coefficient assessment with the intended hen avoids an unexpectedly close mating;
4. **Document the offspring**: young birds are sampled and recorded as soon as they are rung, and when breeding stock is transferred one file travels with each bird, greatly reducing disputes.

**Lesson**: a pedigree is paper evidence; a DNA record is biological evidence. When the two corroborate each other, introductions and transfers become genuinely clear-cut.
