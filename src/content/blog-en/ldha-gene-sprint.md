Short-distance racing in pigeons depends heavily on anaerobic energy supply. During high-speed sprinting and climbing, oxygen supply is relatively insufficient, and the muscles must rely on anaerobic glycolysis to produce ATP quickly in order to sustain the effort. **LDHA (lactate dehydrogenase A)** is a key enzyme in that metabolic pathway.

## The Biological Function of LDHA

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) is an enzyme family made up of several subunits, and LDHA is an important subunit type within it. Under anaerobic conditions, LDHA catalyzes the reduction of pyruvate to lactate while regenerating NAD⁺, allowing glycolysis to continue.

The significance of this process is that when exercise intensity exceeds the limit of aerobic energy supply, anaerobic glycolysis becomes the emergency channel that keeps energy flowing. LDHA activity and expression level directly affect how well a muscle can sustain energy output during intense exercise.

## Gene Polymorphism and Its Association with Flight Performance

Ramadan et al. (2018, PLOS ONE) reported a statistical association between LDHA gene polymorphism and racing survival in pigeons. This association is probabilistic and comes from candidate-gene association research; it is not causal, and no definitive causal link has been established.

It is worth stressing that:

- A genotype indicates a **genetic tendency, not a guarantee**;
- Actual performance is shaped by training, nutrition, health, weather and race format together;
- The effect of a single locus is limited, so several loci should be assessed together.

## Use in Breeding

For pairing speed-type breeding stock, the LDHA genotype can serve as a reference for breeding decisions, always read alongside performance records:

1. **Screening reference**: identify individuals carrying genotypes associated with better anaerobic metabolic efficiency;
2. **Combined assessment**: judge together with loci such as DRD4 (behavior), MSTN (muscle) and LRP8 (energy metabolism);
3. **Avoid relying on one thing alone**: genetic test results should always be read alongside racing records, bloodline and physical condition.

## Scientific Boundaries

To be candid, gene testing in racing pigeons is **candidate-gene testing for performance-related traits**; its scientific value is in providing genetic reference information, not in reading fortunes from DNA. Any claim that testing a gene can predict a champion lacks scientific basis.

For fanciers who want scientific breeding, the value of LDHA and similar testing lies in upgrading breeding decisions from experience alone to a combined judgement of experience plus genetic information, so that pairings can be made more systematically and reproducibly.
