Sex determination in racing pigeons is a foundation of breeding work. Unlike mammals, pigeons show very little difference in appearance between males and females, and traditional methods based on behavior, body size or the appearance of the vent have limited accuracy — with young birds they are almost useless. **DNA sex testing** detects specific gene sequences on the sex chromosomes and delivers highly accurate sex determination from a range of samples, from feathers and blood cards to oral swabs, making it the currently accepted reliable method.

## Why DNA Sex Testing Is Needed

In racing pigeon breeding, accurate sex information directly affects pairing and breeding efficiency. A wrong sex determination can cause failed pairings, waste a breeding season and even lead to disputes in trade. The advantages of DNA testing are:

- **High accuracy**: based on sex-chromosome-specific gene markers, results are stable and reliable;
- **Multiple sample types**: feathers (with the root), blood cards and oral swabs are all acceptable;
- **Young birds can be tested**: sampling is possible after hatching, with no need to wait for sexual maturity;
- **Non-invasive**: sampling causes no harm to the pigeon.

## How the Test Works

Birds use the ZW sex-determination system: males are ZZ and females are ZW. Sex is determined by detecting the presence or absence, or the copy-number difference, of specific genes on the Z and W chromosomes, such as the CHD1 gene. The laboratory amplifies target fragments by PCR and uses electrophoresis or fluorescent quantitative analysis to distinguish ZZ from ZW genotypes.

## Sampling Methods

Sampling is the first step in ensuring reliable results. Recommended sample types and points to note:

- **Feather samples**: pluck 3-5 breast or wing feathers with the root (follicle) intact and place them in a clean zip-seal bag;
- **Blood card samples**: collect 1-2 drops of blood on a dedicated blood card, let it air-dry in the shade, then seal it;
- **Oral swabs**: swab the oral mucosa with a sterile swab, let it dry and place it in a protective tube.

Avoid cross-contamination between samples, and clearly label each sample bag with the pigeon's ring number or ID.

## Reading the Report

The report states clearly "Male (ZZ)" or "Female (ZW)" and includes the loci tested along with quality-control information. On receiving the report, it is advisable to file the sex information together with the pedigree record and ring number as the basis for later pairing decisions.

## How It Differs from Other Genetic Tests

DNA sex testing answers "is this bird male or female," while flight ability gene testing looks at the genetic tendency for traits such as speed, endurance and homing, and a DNA ID builds a unique fingerprint profile for identity verification. The three serve different purposes and can be combined to build a complete genetic record for a racing pigeon.
