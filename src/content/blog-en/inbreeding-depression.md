In racing pigeon breeding, fanciers often use close matings in order to "fix a bloodline" or "purify it by breeding back." Inbreeding, however, is a double-edged sword: while it fixes desirable traits, it also markedly raises the risk that recessive deleterious genes become homozygous, producing **inbreeding depression**.

## What Inbreeding Is

Inbreeding means mating individuals that are closely related. In racing pigeon breeding, common close matings include:

- Father × daughter, mother × son;
- Brother × sister (full siblings);
- Half-sibling matings (same sire, different dam, or the reverse);
- Grandparent × grandoffspring, uncle or aunt × niece or nephew.

## Signs of Inbreeding Depression

Inbreeding depression is the negative effect of inbreeding, typically showing as:

- **Lower fertility**: reduced fertilization and hatch rates;
- **Weak young birds**: slow growth, poor disease resistance, low survival;
- **Genetic defects surfacing**: once recessive deleterious genes are homozygous, the risk of malformation and developmental abnormality rises;
- **Reduced adaptability**: weaker resistance to environmental change and stress;
- **Trait deterioration**: long-term inbreeding can lead to decline in body size and performance.

## The Genetics Behind It

The root cause of inbreeding depression is **homozygosity**:

1. Every individual carries a certain number of recessive deleterious alleles that are not expressed in the heterozygous state;
2. Inbreeding raises the probability that offspring inherit the same allele from a common ancestor;
3. Recessive deleterious genes are expressed when homozygous, which produces the depression;
4. The higher the inbreeding coefficient, the greater the risk of depression.

## A Scientific Approach to Close Breeding

Inbreeding is not an absolute taboo; the key is to use it **in a controlled, purposeful and monitored way**:

1. **Be clear about the aim**: use it only to fix a specific desirable trait or to purify a bloodline;
2. **Limit the degree of inbreeding**: avoid several consecutive generations of intensive inbreeding;
3. **Select strictly**: cull inbred offspring with a weak constitution or poor performance;
4. **Bring in outside blood at the right time**: after purification, introduce unrelated birds to restore heterosis ("hybrid vigor");
5. **Use parentage testing**: DNA parentage testing establishes the true relationships and prevents unintended close matings.

## How Parentage Testing Supports Breeding

The first step in scientific breeding is to know the true relationships. Traditional ring-number records can contain errors or be falsified, whereas DNA parentage testing uses STR microsatellite fingerprints to determine parentage accurately, helping fanciers:

- Confirm the true relationship between breeding stock and offspring;
- Assess the inbreeding coefficient of a proposed pairing;
- Plan a scientific pairing program that balances purification against avoiding depression.

## Case in Practice: After Three "Golden Generations" on One Main Line

**Scenario**: a loft built three generations of close breeding around one outstanding stock cock. The first two generations performed consistently, but from the third generation on, fertilization rates fell, young birds were weak and losses during racing rose.

**Analysis**:

1. **Recognize the signals of inbreeding depression**: falling fertility, poor vitality in young birds and weaker disease resistance are classic signs;
2. **Estimate the inbreeding coefficient**: reviewing the pedigrees showed the third generation's coefficient was already clearly high — after the "golden three generations," outside blood was needed;
3. **Outcross to restore vigor**: introduce unrelated stock whose race records stand up to scrutiny, and pair them with offspring of the main line;
4. **Retain the main line**: breed the outcross offspring back to the main line, balancing fixed strengths against restored vitality;
5. **Keep records**: log hatch rate, weaning weight and health data for every generation, letting data rather than impressions guide how intense the inbreeding should be.

**Lesson**: close breeding is a tool, not a belief. Heavy inbreeding concentrates good genes, and defects as well — only by pacing it with pedigree records and test data can you inbreed without paying the price.
