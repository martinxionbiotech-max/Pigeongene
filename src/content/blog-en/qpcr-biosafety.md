Real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) is currently the accepted reference method in molecular diagnostics, and its sensitivity is extremely high — it can detect pathogen nucleic acid at very low copy numbers. However, **high sensitivity is a double-edged sword**: any minor contamination or handling error can be amplified, leading to false positive or false negative results. Understanding and avoiding these risks is a prerequisite for reliable test results.

## Common Causes of False Positives

A false positive means a positive signal is detected although the target pathogen is not present in the sample. The main causes include:

- **Amplicon contamination**: if PCR products (amplicons) from earlier runs contaminate the laboratory environment, reagents or consumables, they become templates and are amplified again;
- **Cross-contamination**: contact between samples or poor pipetting technique lets a positive sample contaminate a negative one;
- **Aerosol contamination**: opening tubes, centrifuging and similar operations generate nucleic-acid-containing aerosols that drift into the environment.

## Common Causes of False Negatives

A false negative means the target pathogen is present in the sample but is not detected. The main causes include:

- **Sampling problems**: insufficient sample volume, the wrong sampling site, sample degradation;
- **Nucleic acid extraction failure**: low extraction efficiency, residual inhibitors;
- **Reaction inhibition**: impurities in the sample inhibit the PCR reaction;
- **Pathogen load too low**: below the limit of detection.

## How Laboratories Avoid These Problems

Professional laboratories safeguard accuracy through a complete quality control system:

### 1. Zoned Operation

Physically separate the reagent preparation area, sample processing area, amplification area and product analysis area, with one-way flow to prevent amplicons flowing back and causing contamination.

### 2. UNG Anti-Contamination System

Adding uracil-N-glycosylase (UNG) to the reaction system specifically degrades previous amplification products containing dUTP, effectively preventing carry-over contamination.

### 3. Internal Control and Positive Quality Control

An internal control is set up in every reaction to monitor nucleic acid extraction and amplification efficiency, while positive and negative controls verify the reliability of each batch.

### 4. Amplification Efficiency Monitoring

Amplification efficiency is monitored with a standard curve to keep it in the ideal range of 95%-105%, ensuring accurate quantification.

## How Submitters Can Help

- **Follow the sampling rules**: work according to the sampling guide and avoid contact between samples;
- **Store samples properly**: air-dry the sample and seal it, protecting it from moisture, high temperatures and repeated freeze-thaw cycles;
- **Label clearly**: record the pigeon ring number and sample information accurately and avoid recording errors.
